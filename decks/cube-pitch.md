## Review

The deck has a clear route: founder credibility (2), problem (3 to 7), product (8 to 10), proof (11 to 13), market and close (14, 15). The weaknesses are material: five of the first nine slides are text-only statements (5, 6, 7, 8, 11) that carry little visual information, slide 13's ARR chart has no axis labels, years or values, and slides 10 and 12 contain tiny or pixelated elements (blurred G2 badges, very small testimonial and logo text) that cannot be read. **What would change the score:** I would merge or replace some of the sparse statement slides (5 to 8) with a visual or a number, and give the product, traction and market slides more concrete detail.

## Summary

The deck opens with a founder note from Christina Ross, CEO and co-founder, who describes her frustration as a former CFO with slow, consultant-dependent FP&A tools. A short company snapshot follows, listing the founding year, headquarters, employee count, funding and backers. The middle section frames the problem: finance teams are shifting toward strategic roles while their tools lag, and they feel forced to choose between manual spreadsheets and disruptive rip-and-replace systems. Several slides cite the claim that most teams who replace spreadsheets revert to them. The product section presents Cube as a spreadsheet-native FP&A platform built on Excel and Google Sheets, shows a three-step workflow of connecting data, integrating into spreadsheets and pivoting analysis, and notes a short go-live time. Customer logos and an ARR growth slide follow, then a market slide describing a large untapped opportunity. The deck closes with a call to join the company's approach to FP&A.
