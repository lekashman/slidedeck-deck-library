## Review

**6 out of 10, Needs substantial work.** Presentation quality, judged with the SlideDeck Co review heuristic (https://slidedeck.co/review-methodology/). The ask, the evidence and the merit of the idea are not rated.

The deck is short and the route is easy to follow: title, withdrawal of the 2020 targets, disclaimer, leadership, strategy, 2024 targets, then liquidity. Slides 6 and 7 are the strongest pages, with four large colour-coded metrics and a waterfall chart whose highlighted $4.4 total makes the liquidity point easy to find. The weaker pages hold the deck back: slide 2 is an untitled wall of text with half the page empty, slide 3 is a dense small-type disclaimer, and slide 6 pairs the headline numbers with a footnote block too small to read and a large empty lower third. Slide 7 has no takeaway title, and its conditional SoftBank financing is carried only by tiny footnotes, so a reader may not grasp the caveats.

**Worth keeping:** Keep the big-number targets layout on slide 6 and the waterfall with the dashed $4.4 subtotal on slide 7, along with the restrained teal, slate and grey palette and the consistent title and rule treatment.

**Most useful repair:** Give slide 2 a headline that states the message (2020 targets withdrawn, 2024 targets retained) and break the text into short points, and replace the topic titles on slides 6 and 7 with takeaway statements.

**What would change the score:** Surface the key conditions on slide 7 in readable text near the chart, rebalance slide 6 so the numbers fill the page and the footnotes are legible, and add a short note on slide 5 explaining how the timeline links to the stages.

## Summary

The deck is a short March 2020 update from WeWork's parent, The We Company, prepared during the early COVID-19 period. It opens with a title slide and then a forward-looking statement that withdraws earlier 2020 targets. A disclaimer slide follows, covering forward-looking statements, third-party data and non-GAAP measures. The main content sections are the leadership team, a three-stage strategy (short, mid and long term) and the five-year targets for 2024. The closing slide shows pro forma cash and cash commitments as a waterfall chart, tied to SoftBank financing that is subject to conditions. The strongest slides to study are the targets slide, which pairs four large headline metrics with footnoted definitions, and the liquidity waterfall, which shows how each source of cash adds or subtracts from the total.

## Questions about this deck

**What does the WeWork deck say about its 2024 five-year targets?**

On slide 6, WeWork says that by 2024 it expects 1.2M total desks, $7.7B total revenue, $1.8B Adjusted EBITDA and ~$1B free cash flow. The note on that slide says the 2024 figures exclude ChinaCo.

**What does the WeWork deck say about its strategy?**

Slide 5 lays out three stages: a short-term focus (1.0) on delivering the core space-as-a-service offering, a mid-term focus (1.5) on increasing membership offerings and expanding the footprint strategically, and a long-term focus (2.0) on transforming into an end-to-end business solutions platform.

**What does the WeWork deck say about its liquidity and pro forma cash?**

Slide 7 shows a waterfall chart of Pro Forma Cash and Cash Commitments in $ in billions, starting from Available Cash of $1.3 at 12/31/19. The slide notes that SoftBank financing is subject to regulatory approvals and other closing conditions.
