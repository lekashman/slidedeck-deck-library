## Review

**7 out of 10, Effective.** Presentation quality, judged with the SlideDeck Co review heuristic (https://slidedeck.co/review-methodology/). The ask, the evidence and the merit of the idea are not rated.

The route is clear and short: problem (2-4), ideal (5), product (6), demo (7), timing and advantage (8-9), competitors (10-11), tech (12), team (13), model, platform and acquisition (14-16). Slide titles name the point and text is large and legible, so a room can follow it. **Most useful repair:** Move the footer watermark clear of body text on slides 6, 10 and 16, and tone down or remove the large box watermark behind the bullets.

**What would change the score:** Replace the closing quote on slide 17 with a titled closing slide, and show the demo on slide 7 as a full-size annotated screenshot instead of a small one under a giant label.

## Summary

The deck opens with a single-line title slide, then frames the problem: in 2007, storing, syncing, sharing and backing up files remains painful. It reviews current workarounds, describes the ideal experience, and includes a short demo placeholder. Next it argues that the timing is right and that this approach beats existing options, comparing competitors such as Carbonite, Mozy, box.net and Foldershare across sync, backup, sharing, versioning and OS integration. A section on technical advantages follows, covering the Python stack, use of Amazon S3 and EC2, and a performance focus. The team slide introduces the two founders and their backgrounds. Later sections cover the business model for individuals and small businesses, a platform strategy for moving content from the desktop to web apps, and customer acquisition through early adopters, free accounts and viral sharing. The deck closes with a quoted endorsement from a startup ranking. The problem and workaround slides, which build the case in short bullet lists, and the competitor capability grid are worth studying.

**What business model does the Dropbox deck describe?**

Slide 14 says individuals get a freemium model and small and medium businesses get a shared folder per-seat license that replaces backup, Windows file share, and collaboration tools. It also describes a platform strategy to broker all user-generated content from the desktop to the web.

**What technical advantages does the Dropbox deck list?**
