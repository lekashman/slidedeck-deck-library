## Review

**7 out of 10, Effective.** Presentation quality, judged with the SlideDeck Co review heuristic (https://slidedeck.co/review-methodology/). The ask, the evidence and the merit of the idea are not rated.

The deck makes its argument clearly through a strong route: seven aspects, an agenda slide that returns with the current section highlighted (4, 19, 40), and a pacing of one idea per slide that suits a long read-through. The Enron framing on slides 5 to 7 is memorable, and the growth-complexity-talent-density build on slides 43 to 59 is a disciplined, easy-to-follow line diagram sequence. Weaknesses are consequential: the deck is plain black text on white with almost no visual identity beyond a small logo, and the nine-behaviour slides (10 to 18) are repetitive, small-text, unevenly placed and sit at the top or middle of the page with large empty areas. Slide 1 is an archive notice rather than a title, and the 125-page length with many near-duplicate pages (25/26, 37/38, 70/71) dilutes pace.

**What would change the score:** I would give the deck a stronger visual system, such as colour bands or numbered section dividers and larger key phrases, so that the long run of plain text slides has rhythm and emphasis.

## Summary

This internal culture deck, later archived in favour of a newer version on the jobs site, opens with a title and a freedom-and-responsibility section divider, then states that the culture aims at excellence. Its core is a list of seven culture aspects: values, high performance, freedom and responsibility, context over control, highly aligned but loosely coupled teams, top-of-market pay, and promotion and development. Each aspect gets its own section, and the deck contrasts stated corporate values with the behaviours actually rewarded. Early slides use a framing device: a list of nice-sounding values followed by a slide showing the same values displayed at Enron, used to argue that rewards reveal real values. Nine named behaviours follow in a grid, with one slide per behaviour and short bullet points beneath. The deck then moves through talent density, the keeper test, freedom as companies grow, context versus control, compensation practices and career growth. It closes with a sequence of recurring section headers and a short line about continuing to improve the culture as the company grows.
