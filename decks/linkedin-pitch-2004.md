## Review

**7 out of 10, Effective.** Presentation quality, judged with the SlideDeck Co review heuristic (https://slidedeck.co/review-methodology/). The ask, the evidence and the merit of the idea are not rated.

The argument runs in a clear order: the 1.0 versus 2.0 framing (slides 4 to 8), the network-first priority, market leadership, three products with market sizing, pricing, financials, team, and a close. Action-style titles and a consistent header, logo and footer make it easy to follow, and the repeated paired-box layout on slides 5 to 8 makes the central idea memorable. The weaknesses are consequential: slide 33 is a tiny, dense spreadsheet with no emphasis on the profitability claim in its title, the yellow-filled charts on slides 10 and 11 have unreadable axis text, and slide 14 has an overlapping footer and crowded text. Several slides (2, 21, 25, 28) use small screenshots and boilerplate boxes, and the closing slide 37 is a left-aligned layout with a small graphic that looks unfinished.

**Worth keeping:** Keep the repeated 1.0 versus 2.0 paired-box device on slides 5 to 8 and the consistent action-title header system.

**Most useful repair:** Rebuild slide 33 as a larger table or chart with the operating margin line highlighted, and clean up slide 14 and the yellow chart backgrounds on slides 10 and 11.

**What would change the score:** I would enlarge or crop the screenshots on slides 23, 25 and 28 to show one feature each, and cut the sparse text on slides 9 and 36 into a more visual form.

## Summary

The deck opens with a title slide and the claim that LinkedIn is "Professional People Search 2.0," contrasting networked search with flat directories. It draws parallels to eBay, PayPal and Google, each shown as a 1.0 versus 2.0 comparison. Next comes the problem, the network-first growth strategy, and competitive position against rival professional networks. Three revenue products follow: InLeads (targeted search ads), Opportunities (network-filtered job listings) and Network Plus (a paid subscription extending reach). Each product receives market sizing with comparables and charts of online advertising and job market revenue. The middle section covers pricing, launch timing, the business model and margins, and a five-year financial table projecting operating profitability in 2005. The deck closes with the team, the board and investors, and a short summary slide. Slide 5 is worth studying for its side-by-side 1.0 versus 2.0 layout that makes the network argument at a glance. Slide 33 reduces the financials to a single table with a clear operating margin line. Slides 12 and 13 show market share over time with a simple pie-style chart and a one-line takeaway.
