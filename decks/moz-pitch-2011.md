## Review

**6 out of 10, Needs substantial work.** Presentation quality, judged with the SlideDeck Co review heuristic (https://slidedeck.co/review-methodology/). The ask, the evidence and the merit of the idea are not rated.

The route is easy to follow: history (2-3), growth model (4-5), market (7-10), problem (11-14), customers (15-18), metrics (19-21), raise (22-23), risks (24-28), use of funds (29-33), and a close (34-36). Most content slides use short, large labels in a consistent branded template, and the metric slides 20, 21 and 23 are quick to read. The visual execution is uneven, though. Slide 3 stacks tiny chart labels under crossing arrows, slide 16 has small text in an overlapping Venn, and slides 25-28 rely on decorative images (a cartoon, a Facebook sign, a politician photo) with no visible explanation. The metrics and risk slides also lack comparison or context, and the section-divider pages (4, 7, 11, 15, 19, 22, 24, 29, 34) add length without carrying content.

**Worth keeping:** Keep the large, scannable label-and-value format of slides 20 and 21, the framed pie chart with two callouts on slide 9, and the three-column tool frequency layout on slide 13.

**Most useful repair:** Rework slides 3 and 16 so the chart and diagram labels are readable, and replace the risk images on slides 25-28 with a stated risk and its consequence, since the footer bars there are empty.

**What would change the score:** Cut or merge the divider slides, and make slide 23 and the use-of-funds pages show how the money is allocated rather than a list of lines.

## Summary

The deck opens on a title slide naming Rand Fishkin as CEO and co-founder and setting the goal of becoming Seattle's next $1 billion company. It then gives a short company history and the funding raised to date, and explains how the company grew through inbound and organic marketing rather than paid acquisition. A market section follows, covering macroeconomic trends, the under-investment in organic marketing, and the analytics problems marketers face. Next, the deck defines the problems the product solves and the target customer segments. A metrics section reports 2011 revenue, subscribers, trial conversion, churn, margins and profit. The deck then describes the planned raise of $20 to $25 million, proposed board composition, business risks, use of funds, and a two-year team roadmap. It closes with potential acquisitions and a short case for why the company is positioned to win, ending with a call to join the company. Slides 3, 9 and 21 show how key numbers are presented as direct, scannable statements, and slide 13 turns a complex tool landscape into one clear argument.
