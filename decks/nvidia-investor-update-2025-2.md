## Review

**7 out of 10, Effective.** Presentation quality, judged with the SlideDeck Co review heuristic (https://slidedeck.co/review-methodology/). The ask, the evidence and the merit of the idea are not rated.

The deck has a clear route from NVIDIA's shift to an AI infrastructure company, through the market case, platform, physical AI and annual roadmap, to the OpenAI partnership, and it is built with consistent branding, green accents and a generally legible template. Slides 10 and 12 are strong: the roadmap grid on slide 10 organizes a lot of product detail by year and layer, and the MLPerf chart on slide 12 makes one comparison immediately readable. The weaknesses are consequential. Slide 4 packs small bullet text beside a chart whose point is partly lost, and slides 5 to 9 reuse the title '$3-4 Trillion AI Infrastructure Spend by 2030' on content that is not about spend, so titles stop carrying the message. Slides 13 and 14 have tiny text or chart labels and uneven spacing, slide 3's timeline labels are very small, and the deck ends on a logo slide rather than a takeaway.

**Worth keeping:** Keep the slide 10 roadmap grid, the slide 12 chart with its single clear contrast, the consistent NVIDIA green and black palette, and the strong imagery on slides 5 and 7 to 9.

**Most useful repair:** Rewrite titles on slides 5 to 9 and 11 to 13 so each states that page's actual claim, and cut and enlarge the slide 4 bullets and the slide 13 and 14 text to a readable size.

**What would change the score:** I would split the slide 4 bullets from the chart, label the chart's key values, and end the deck on a summary slide instead of the logo page.

## Summary

The deck opens with a title slide and a long forward-looking statements disclaimer, then sets out NVIDIA's shift from chips to an AI infrastructure company. The middle sections cover the market case, citing $3-4 trillion of AI infrastructure spend by 2030 and growth drivers across cloud, enterprise and physical AI, then show the CUDA-X platform stack and token-usage growth. Further sections cover physical AI computing (training, simulation and deployment across robotics, autonomous vehicles and factories), an annual product roadmap from Blackwell through Rubin to Feynman, MLPerf benchmark wins, and a partnership with OpenAI for at least 10 gigawatts of infrastructure. The deck closes on that OpenAI partnership slide, which sets out the investment terms and spend per gigawatt. Slides 4, 10 and 12 are worth studying for how they use dense but structured data, a roadmap grid and benchmark charts to carry the argument.
