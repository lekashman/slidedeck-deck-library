## Review

**6 out of 10, Needs substantial work.** Presentation quality, judged with the SlideDeck Co review heuristic (https://slidedeck.co/review-methodology/). The ask, the evidence and the merit of the idea are not rated.

The deck has a clear, short route (trend, problem question, product, traction, milestones, model, market, proof, standard, competition, team, contact) with one idea per slide, large readable type and a consistent logo and texture. But the execution is plain: slides 5, 7, 8 and 12 are bulleted text, slide 4 uses a tiny unreadable screenshot, and the key numbers are not set up as visual evidence. Slide 5 places bullets over a growth curve with no axes and the line runs through the text, slide 6 crowds the footer logo and mixes past and future milestones in one undifferentiated list, and slide 11 is cluttered with mismatched logos and a duplicated Buffer mark.

**Worth keeping:** Keep the one-message-per-slide pacing, the large bold headings, the consistent logo footer and the labelled category circles on slide 11.

**Most useful repair:** Remove the curve from behind the bullets on slide 5 (or turn it into a labelled chart with a few big numbers), and separate achieved from projected milestones on slide 6 with clear spacing and a visible timeline.

**What would change the score:** I would replace the text-only slides 7 and 8 with a few large figures or a simple diagram, show a legible product screen on slide 4 and add a clear ask slide before the contact page.

## Summary

The deck opens with two quotes framing social sharing as the most important trend in marketing, then asks how social can drive traffic. It moves to the product idea of queuing updates, followed by traction figures: paying users, annual revenue run rate, margins and user growth. A milestones slide lays out the timeline from web app launch through API launch and integrations to projected user and revenue targets. The business model slide covers freemium conversion, churn and customer acquisition economics, and the social media landscape slide gives market context on sharing volume and traffic. A slide on the effect of buffering cites a click increase, and later slides cover the plan to become a default sharing standard and a competition map sorted into categories. The deck closes with the team and advisors, then a contact slide. Slides worth studying are the traction slide, which uses short numeric bullets, the milestones timeline, and the competition slide, which groups rivals into labelled categories.

## Questions about this deck

**What does the Buffer deck say about its traction?**

Slide 5 lists 800 paying users, a $150,000 annual revenue run rate, 97% margins, 55,000 users growing 40% per month, and 1.5 million updates buffered. These figures are the traction metrics the deck presents.

**How many slides does the Buffer pitch deck have?**

The Buffer deck has 13 slides. It opens with a title slide and closes with a contact slide on slide 13.

**What does the Buffer deck say about its business model?**

Slide 7 describes a freemium model with a consistent 2% conversion from Free to Paid plans. It also states that 5% churn equates to an LTV of $240 and allows the company to pay up to $5 to acquire a free user.

**How does the Buffer deck structure its competition slide?**

Slide 11 groups competitors into labelled categories such as Social Media Dashboards, Intelligent Sharing, Sharing Platforms, and Scheduling Apps. The slide uses logo collages inside circular label bubbles to show them.
