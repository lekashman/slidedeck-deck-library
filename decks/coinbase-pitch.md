## Review

**6 out of 10, Needs substantial work.** Presentation quality, judged with the SlideDeck Co review heuristic (https://slidedeck.co/review-methodology/). The ask, the evidence and the merit of the idea are not rated.

The deck follows a clear short route (what bitcoin is, market growth, the usability problem, the product, the iTunes analogy, Coinbase's own traction) with one idea per slide and large, readable headlines. The slide 8 analogy and the slide 9 and 10 charts with big callouts ('20% Daily', '$65,000 USD in the first 5 weeks') are memorable and easy to read in a spoken pitch. The weaknesses are real, though: slides 1, 6 and 7 crop the product screenshot at the bottom edge, the slide 6 'current tools' comparison is too small to read so the difficulty claim is asserted rather than shown, and slides 2 and 3 repeat the same title with a decorative globe that explains nothing. Chart axes on slides 4, 9 and 10 are tiny and pasted from a dashboard, and slide 10 shows BTC volume while the callout says USD, which blurs the message.

**Worth keeping:** Keep the one-idea-per-slide pacing, the Coinbase : Bitcoin / iTunes : MP3 analogy on slide 8, and the large callout numbers on the growth charts.

**Most useful repair:** Fix the cropped screenshots on slides 1, 6 and 7, enlarge or annotate the current-tools images on slide 6, and make the chart axes and units on slides 4, 9 and 10 legible and consistent with the callouts.

**What would change the score:** I would merge or differentiate slides 2 and 3, and replace the word-cloud on slide 5 with sized, clearer use cases, since the varying font sizes imply a ranking that is not explained.

## Summary

The deck opens with a title slide introducing Coinbase as a hosted bitcoin wallet, then moves to a short overview of bitcoin as a new digital currency, highlighting instant, international transfers with no transaction fees. It then shows early market traction, with bitcoin transaction volume of about $2 million USD per day, and lists use cases such as peer-to-peer and international payments, micro-transactions, virtual goods, games, e-commerce and remittance. The problem is framed as current tools being too difficult to use, which sets up Coinbase as a simpler hosted wallet. A comparison slide likens Coinbase to iTunes for MP3s, positioning it as a consumer-friendly layer on a new format. Growth slides show daily signups increasing about 20% per day and roughly $65,000 USD in transactions during the first five weeks. The deck closes by describing Coinbase as a new payment network based on the digital currency bitcoin. The structure is short and sparse, with one idea per slide and simple headline-style titles.
