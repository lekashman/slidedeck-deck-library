## Review

**6 out of 10, Needs substantial work.** Presentation quality, judged with the SlideDeck Co review heuristic (https://slidedeck.co/review-methodology/). The ask, the evidence and the merit of the idea are not rated.

The route is clear and the one-line statements on slides 1, 3 and 7 give the story a confident rhythm: a hook, a problem, a solution, then proof. But the middle pages are weak. The problem slides 4 to 6 use a decorative red ring that carries no meaning and small grey bullet text, and slides 11 to 14 split one product overview across five pages with tiny text and no visual explanation. Slide 15 is the strongest explanatory page, but its body copy is small, low-contrast and soft, and the final traction pages (17 to 19) have unlabeled axes and a small, gray-heavy slide 19, so the evidence is harder to read than the story requires.

**Worth keeping:** Keep the short declarative slides (1, 3, 7) and the Develop, Preview, Ship structure with its chat-thread example on slide 15, which makes the workflow concrete.

**Most useful repair:** Make the text on slides 4 to 6, 12 to 15 and 19 larger and darker, replace the meaningless red rings with a real visual, label the chart axes on slides 17 and 18, and fix the low-resolution images on slides 2, 9 and 15.

**What would change the score:** I would collapse slides 11 to 14 into one clear product diagram, drop the dim logo wall behind the stats on slide 9, and give slide 19 a single headline takeaway instead of a dense table beside three charts.

## Summary

The deck opens with a tweet from a developer praising Vercel and Next.js, then frames a problem: frontend teams are slowed by disjointed development processes, difficult production infrastructure, and repeated regressions that stop teams reusing past work. It then presents Vercel as the solution, introduces Next.js with adoption figures, and describes the platform in three parts: production infrastructure, an intelligent edge network, and cross-functional collaboration. A slide on the Develop, Preview and Ship workflow walks through an example deploy preview reviewed by engineering, design and marketing teams. The closing section shows enterprise go-to-market traction and charts of paid product usage and Next.js adoption, with month-over-month growth figures. Several slides are short, one-line statements that carry the argument, and the charts are used to back the growth story.
