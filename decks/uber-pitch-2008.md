## Review

**5 out of 10, Needs substantial work.** Presentation quality, judged with the SlideDeck Co review heuristic (https://slidedeck.co/review-methodology/). The ask, the evidence and the merit of the idea are not rated.

The story route is easy to follow (problem on slides 2-3, concept on 4-6, features, market, outcomes, progress on 25), and the text is large and legible. But nearly every page is a default bulleted list with a centered title, so there is no hierarchy beyond title and bullets and no visual rhythm across 25 slides. Several visuals are weak: the medallion chart on slide 3 is tiny, the slide 17 table is a scanned-looking page with no highlighted figure, and the slides 21 and 24 smartphone charts are unreadable and loosely tied to their points. The logo is a blurry thumbnail on every slide, and slide 25 leaves most of the page empty. The core can be recovered but substantial revision is needed.

**Worth keeping:** Keep the plain problem-to-solution-to-progress order, the large readable bullet text, and the green accent on the slide 3 takeaway and the NEXT line on slide 25.

**Most useful repair:** Replace the pasted tables and charts on slides 3, 17, 19, 21 and 24 with one clean, enlarged chart or a single big number each, and swap the blurry logo for a crisp version.

**What would change the score:** I would turn the bullet lists into assertion titles with one visual per slide, such as the app screens or a trip flow, and merge the thin slides (7, 14, 22, 23) so the deck gets a clear rhythm.

## Summary

The deck opens by framing the state of San Francisco and New York taxi service in 2008, citing aging dispatch technology, fuel-inefficient vehicles and the medallion system as the problem. It then introduces UberCab as a members-only, one-click, on-demand car service with a geo-aware app and a luxury hybrid fleet. The middle sections cover key differentiators, operating principles, app and web features, use cases, user and environmental benefits, fleet and service area, technology, demand forecasting and market size. Later slides estimate the market, list target cities, set out best-case, realistic and worst-case outcomes, and sketch future optimizations and marketing ideas. The deck closes with a progress-to-date slide listing completed steps and the next milestones of buying cars, building the app and a February demo. Slides worth studying include the problem slide with its bold statistics, the market size slide with its single headline figure, and the progress slide that shows what has been done so far.

## Questions about this deck

**How many slides does the UberCab deck have?**

The UberCab deck has 25 slides. Slide 25 is the Progress to Date slide, which lists completed steps and the next milestones.

**What does the UberCab deck say about the market size?**

Slide 17 states that the UberCab overall market is $4.2B annually and growing, and that the top 4 players combined only hold 22% of revenues. Slide 19 lists the 2009 Taxi and Limousine Service figures for individual US cities.

**What potential outcomes does the UberCab deck describe?**

Slide 20 describes a best-case scenario in which UberCab becomes the market leader with $1B+ in yearly revenue. Its realistic success scenario is to get 5% of the top 5 US cities and generate 20-30M+ per year profit.

**What fuel efficiency does the UberCab fleet deck give?**

Slide 13 says the premium fleet includes the S550 for SF Beta clients and gets 30mpg with the S400 BlueHybrid (in 2010). The standard fleet of Lexus GS450h or e320 gets 23mpg, while existing cab and towncar fleets get 14-16 mpg.
