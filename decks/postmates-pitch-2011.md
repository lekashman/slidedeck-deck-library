## Review

**6 out of 10, Needs substantial work.** Presentation quality, judged with the SlideDeck Co review heuristic (https://slidedeck.co/review-methodology/). The ask, the evidence and the merit of the idea are not rated.

The core case comes through: a clear comparison table (5), a market chart with a plain takeaway (6) and a four-phase rollout (9) let a pitch audience follow the story from problem to market to plan. Several weaker pages hold it back. Slide 4 is a bare demo link with a password, slide 3 is a single photo with no message, slide 7 is two tiny unranked bullet lists, and the closing slide 13 is the title slide again, shifted to the left side. Slide 5 has inconsistent rows (USPS all dashes, two Independent Courier rows with no distinction) and slide 9 has white text on light gray that is hard to read, so the best slides carry visible flaws.

**Worth keeping:** Keep the slide 5 table with its green Postmates row and red problem cells, the slide 6 chart with its red callout, and the plain, consistent title style.

**Most useful repair:** Fix the readability of the white-on-gray deliveries lines on slide 9, label the two courier rows on slide 5 and explain the USPS dashes, and enlarge or group the bullet lists on slide 7.

**What would change the score:** Replace the bare demo and photo slides with pages that state a point, make slide 2 larger and better filled, and end on a real closing slide instead of a repeated title page.

## Summary

The deck opens with a title slide introducing Postmates as an on-demand local delivery service, followed by a team and company overview listing the founders, a $125K seed round, and early investors and advisors. It then moves to a live demo and a comparison table showing how Postmates weighs cost, tracking, time and effort against USPS, UPS, independent couriers, U-Haul and driving oneself. Market sizing comes next, with a chart of the roughly $72 billion US courier market and a note on its fragmentation, followed by a slide on adjacent markets Postmates could enter. Traction, a phased market discovery-to-lock plan, the business model and the technical architecture follow in sequence. The deck closes with a short summary of the opportunity, the San Francisco trial and the team, and repeats the title slide. Slides 5, 6 and 9 are the most useful to study for how comparison, market sizing and staged rollout are shown.

**What traction does the Postmates deck report for its trial?**

**How does the Postmates deck compare its cost and speed with other shipping options?**

Slide 5 compares options for a 43x30x30" item shipped from San Francisco to Palo Alto. Postmates is shown at $60 with tracking, delivery Now, and 5mins of effort, while UPS is shown at $220 with Next Day delivery and an independent courier at $492 for Same Day delivery.
