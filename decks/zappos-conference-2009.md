## Review

**5 out of 10, Needs substantial work.** Presentation quality, judged with the SlideDeck Co review heuristic (https://slidedeck.co/review-methodology/). The ask, the evidence and the merit of the idea are not rated.

The deck has a recognisable spine (background, three Cs, culture and values, happiness research, closing question) and big, legible text that works for a spoken talk. But many slides are bare word cards or stock-style pictures with no support (2, 5, 11, 19, 20, 22, 34), so the argument lives almost entirely in the speaker, and the shift from Zappos to the happiness frameworks (24-31) is only loosely connected. Execution flaws weigh further: pure default blue on white, a sales chart with a stray artifact and no stated unit or label for gross sales (4), text colliding with the footer (33), duplicate and empty end slides (37-39), and an uncaptioned pizza image repeated without explanation.

**Worth keeping:** The pyramid on slide 9, the simple smiley-and-arrow progression on slides 31 and 35, the bold quote on slide 10, and the large chart with the data table on slide 4 are the clearest carriers of the message.

**Most useful repair:** Fix the overflow on slide 33, remove the chart artifact and add a unit label on slide 4, and delete the empty or redundant slides 37 to 39 and the repeated pizza slide.

**What would change the score:** I would replace the single-word cards with slides that state a point, add a clear section marker and takeaway for each of the three Cs and each happiness framework, and drop the saturated blue for a restrained palette that echoes the brand.

## Summary

The deck opens with a title slide for Tony Hsieh, Zappos CEO, presenting at Startup School on October 24, 2009, followed by a short personal background covering his early ventures and the sale of LinkExchange. It then moves to the company itself, showing a bar chart of gross sales from 2000 to 2008 under the heading about the power of repeat customers, and frames the business around three Cs: clothing, customer service and culture. The middle section develops culture and core values, including a list of committable values, and argues that vision and purpose inspire more than money. A later section draws on research into happiness, presenting three types of happiness (pleasure, passion and higher purpose) and linking them to business outcomes. The deck closes by repeating the question of what the company's higher purpose is, with the title Delivering Happiness and a contact slide. Slides worth studying include the sales chart, the three-Cs pyramid, and the three-types-of-happiness diagram, which uses simple graphics to carry the argument.
