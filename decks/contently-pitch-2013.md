## Review

**7 out of 10, Effective.** Presentation quality, judged with the SlideDeck Co review heuristic (https://slidedeck.co/review-methodology/). The ask, the evidence and the merit of the idea are not rated.

The deck has a clear, readable route: market claim (2), problem (3), product (5 to 7), traction (8, 9), strategy (10 to 12), team (13). Most slides carry one large headline in a consistent teal system with a cyan logo tab, so the message reaches the audience quickly. Weaknesses are consequential: the title slide (1) shows placeholder black boxes instead of client logos, and slides 8, 9 and 11 are heavily blacked out with 'secret sauce' and 'secret plans' labels, so the chart on 8 and the pipeline graphic on 9 communicate little. ## Summary

The deck opens with a Series B financing title slide and then frames the market with a 2013 claim that content marketing is brands' top digital priority, followed by a survey chart showing creating original content as the leading challenge for marketers. A quote from Forbes positions Contently as platform infrastructure for brand publishing. The core product section shows a two-part offering: a network of more than 20,000 vetted freelance journalists and a cloud-based content creation software suite, illustrated with a journalist profile and a workflow screen. The middle sections cover company metrics in a revenue chart, customer pipeline value, and a strategy slide describing the aim to own the full value chain from talent to creation to distribution. A roadmap slide lays out the Series A and Series B timeline and planned expansion areas. The deck closes with a snapshot of founders, investors, key employees, advisors, technology stack and capital raised to date, ending on a short tagline slide. Several figures and plans are redacted, which limits how much of the financial case can be read from the text.
