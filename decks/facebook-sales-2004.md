## Review

**5 out of 10, Needs substantial work.** Presentation quality, judged with the SlideDeck Co review heuristic (https://slidedeck.co/review-methodology/). The ask, the evidence and the merit of the idea are not rated.

The deck has a clear route from what the site is, to the audience, to ad services and a contact, and the blue identity is consistent. But slide 2 is a leftover template layout page with a note to modify the template, and slide 16's gender and age icons (a stall-like shape, a bare outline, a figure with a keg) do not read as men, women or age, so the one-figure-per-slide idea fails on the key page. Many slides are small text on blue with large empty areas (6, 7, 20, 23, 24), the pies on 14 and 15 have tiny labels and no emphasis, and the sales slides never connect the audience numbers to ad value.

**Worth keeping:** Keep the consistent blue grid background and wordmark, the large single-figure approach on slides 17 and 18, and the plain launch-date tables on slides 10 and 11.

**Most useful repair:** Delete slide 2, replace the slide 16 icons with recognisable symbols or large percentages, and enlarge text and chart labels on slides 7, 14, 15, 23 and 24.

**What would change the score:** Give slides action titles that state the point (for example growth, reach, targeting) and add a real growth chart in place of the text on slide 20.

## Summary

The deck opens with a cover and a quoted press clipping from The Stanford Daily describing how the site has swept through campus. It then explains what thefacebook.com is, an online directory linking students, alumni, faculty and staff at colleges. Next it covers the user profile, the intra-school and inter-school networking features, and the school launch timeline in two tables, one for Ivy League schools and one for others. The audience section sizes the college market and its spending, followed by a series of user demographics and site usage slides with figures from March 2004. A user growth slide describes the trend in plain terms. The sales part follows, covering online marketing services, targeted advertising parameters, ad sizes and rates. It closes with a contact slide naming the company's CFO and a second press quote from The Harvard Independent. Slides worth studying include the demographic breakdown slides, which use one large figure per slide, and the school launch tables, which present a timeline as a simple grid.
