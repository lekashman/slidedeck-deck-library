## Review

**6 out of 10, Needs substantial work.** Presentation quality, judged with the SlideDeck Co review heuristic (https://slidedeck.co/review-methodology/). The ask, the evidence and the merit of the idea are not rated.

The route is clear and conventional (problem, market, solution, model, marketing, competition, traction, team, projections, ask), and most slides carry one action-style takeaway line with arrows, so an investor can follow the case quickly. Execution is uneven: the title slide has a low-contrast logo on dark grey, slide 11 reproduces a dense spreadsheet screenshot that is too small and blurry to read, and slides 8 and 9 use tiny thumbnails and logos that add little. Slides 3, 7 and 12 are readable and the slide 7 positioning axis with a use-case grid is a strong idea, but the deck swings between black and grey backgrounds with stock icons and clip-art, and the ask is folded into the projections slide, so substantial revision is needed.

**Worth keeping:** Keep the slide 7 use-case grid and difficult-to-easy axis, the one-line takeaway under each slide, and the simple three-column structure of slides 3 and 4.

**Most useful repair:** Replace the slide 11 screenshot with a short readable summary of revenue, costs and profit by year, give the ask its own slide with the amount, and raise the contrast of the slide 1 logo.

**What would change the score:** I would unify the background colour and visual style, enlarge or drop the small thumbnails on slides 8 and 9, and remove the stock icons and clip-art in favour of real product and data visuals.

## Summary

The deck opens with a title slide naming Revolut and its founder, then moves into a problem statement: sending and spending money abroad is expensive, inconvenient and hidden in fees. It sizes the market with a sequence of figures on cross-currency transaction volume, fees lost to spreads, and travellers and expats, before narrowing to a UK initial target. The solution is shown as three verbs (exchange, send, spend) with a short explanatory video link. The business model section sets out a freemium ladder of Free, Basic and Pro tiers alongside a viral referral mechanic. Marketing, competition, differentiators, traction, team, financial projections and the funding ask follow in order. The closing slide lists use of funds across hiring, growing the freemium base and preparing a Series A. Several slides are worth studying: the business model slide shows how three tiers are compared in a single layout, the competition slide frames rivals through customer use cases rather than feature checklists, and the projections slide lays out a five-year table with a clear summary of revenue, costs and profit.
