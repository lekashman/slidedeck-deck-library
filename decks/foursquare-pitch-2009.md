## Review

**6 out of 10, Needs substantial work.** Presentation quality, judged with the SlideDeck Co review heuristic (https://slidedeck.co/review-methodology/). The ask, the evidence and the merit of the idea are not rated.

The route is clear and ordered: definition on slide 2, then one product feature per page (slides 3 to 10), then revenue on slides 11 and 12, with real phone screenshots that show the product honestly. The weaknesses are consequential. Every slide uses the same plain white template with no color system, and slides 11 and 12 reuse one badge screenshot that has nothing to do with the revenue text beside it. Slide 6 is a blurry Twitter screenshot with tiny, low-contrast text and no stated takeaway. Slide 2 is a dense wall of small text, slide 14 is a blurry unlabeled dashboard with no caption, and slide 15 is a plain trivia list, so the close is weak.

**Worth keeping:** Keep the one-feature-per-slide sequence with a real phone screenshot beside a short caption (slides 3, 4, 5, 8, 10), the large readable titles, and the concrete examples in slide 12.

**Most useful repair:** Replace the repeated badge screenshot on slides 11 and 12 with a visual that shows the revenue idea, enlarge or recrop the slide 6 tweets and add a one-line takeaway, and label the slide 14 dashboard so the audience knows what the numbers mean.

**What would change the score:** I would break up the text wall on slide 2, give the deck a consistent color and accent system beyond the logo, and end on a clear summary or next step instead of the trivia list on slide 15.

## Summary

The deck opens by defining foursquare as a mix of friend-finder, social city guide and social game, then explains that it runs on website, iPhone app, SMS and API. It moves through the core product in order: friend finding, check-ins as the basic action, points and mayorships, a leaderboard, tips as short recommendations, and badges tied to time and place. Real examples of local merchants rewarding their mayors appear as proof that venues already use the mechanic. The middle section turns to business, setting lead generation as the primary revenue model and listing secondary streams such as sponsored badges, redeemable points and banner placements. It closes with a sample user profile showing 30-day stats and a trivia slide covering city coverage, platforms, the API beta and the two-person team. Slides 6, 11 and 12 are the most worth studying: slide 6 shows how customer quotes can carry a product story, slide 11 frames revenue around a measurable offer metric, and slide 12 gives concrete examples for each revenue idea.

## Questions about this deck

**What does the foursquare deck say is its primary revenue model?**

Slide 11 states that lead generation will be foursquare's primary revenue model, with incremental revenue from sponsorships of points and badges and from resale of data aggregated from user behavior.

**How many slides does the foursquare presentation deck have and how is it organized?**

The foursquare deck has 15 slides. Slides 2 through 5 cover the core product and its first examples, slides 6 through 9 cover mayors, leaderboard and tips, and slide 15 closes with trivia about cities, platforms, the API beta and the team.

**What platforms does foursquare run on according to its deck?**

Slide 2 says foursquare is currently a website, an iPhone application, an SMS service and an API. Slide 15 adds a mobile website for Blackberry and Android users and an SMS shortcode for older devices.

**What example does the foursquare deck give for a sponsored badge?**

Slide 12 gives the example of checking in at three Starbucks to unlock a Dunkin' Donuts badge that can be redeemed for a free hot chocolate. It also gives a Red Bull Late Night badge for check-ins after 3am.
