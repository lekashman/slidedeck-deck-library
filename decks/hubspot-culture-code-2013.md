## Review

**8 out of 10, Strong.** Presentation quality, judged with the SlideDeck Co review heuristic (https://slidedeck.co/review-methodology/). The ask, the evidence and the merit of the idea are not rated.

The deck holds a consistent system across the pages I saw: a dark charcoal ground, an orange accent period, bold-versus-light weight to mark the key word, and large numerals (slides 14, 33, 45, 55) that signal each of the seven sections. **What would change the score:** I would replace the generic or off-tone stock photos (10, 14, 29) with HubSpot's own team imagery as on slide 17 and trim near-duplicate build slides such as 5 and 6, 31 and 32, and 34 and 35.

## Summary

The deck opens with a title slide and then defines company culture as shared beliefs, values and practices, framing HubSpot's Culture Code as the operating system of the company. It then argues why culture matters for recruiting, contrasts how work has changed from past to present, and presents the seven points of the code in order: mission and metrics, solving for the customer, transparency, autonomy and ownership, people as the best perk, daring to be different, and recognising that life is short. Each principle gets its own section with short headline slides, quotes, and a few policy examples, and the deck is candid about gaps such as limited diversity and first-time managers. It closes with a recap of the seven principles, a line inviting feedback, and a thank-you slide pointing to the open discussion site. Slides worth studying include the opening definition slide, the then-and-now comparison table, and the one-idea-per-slide sequence on transparency. The deck is long, with many slides carrying a single line of text on a dark background.
